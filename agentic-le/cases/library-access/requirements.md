# Understand

PROBLEM FRAME
Investigate what enables adults to choose an acceptable next action for an unfamiliar service while retaining control, privacy and manageable effort. Barriers may lie in service design, available support, language or device access. Independent online completion is not the objective. A reasonable outcome can be seeking help, checking availability, leaving a plan undecided or stopping.

ADOPTED REVISION
The draft interaction must include “availability unknown,” “none works for me,” and “pause or stop,” without requiring a reason. Next actions may be selected, spoken, written or undecided. This changes both the interaction and its review criteria: a finished plan is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of supported choice. These are working requirements awaiting human review, not approved commitments.

FICTIONAL REVIEW MATERIAL
Exercise text: “Imagine a made-up library event called Community Drawing Hour. Its example notice offers morning or afternoon attendance and says ‘confirm your place’ without explaining how. This exercise cannot register anyone. Online, paper and staffed routes are possibilities to examine; their real availability is unknown.”

Planning card, usable on paper or as a later screen outline:
• Approach: online / check for paper / check for staffed help / availability unknown / none works / pause or stop.
• Next action: examine the fictional notice / ask what ‘confirm’ means if help is available / check whether another route exists / leave undecided / stop. A different fictional action may be expressed, but writing is optional.
• Change prompt: “The shared computer session ends before you continue, or the instruction remains confusing. Keep, change or abandon your plan.” Do not require recovery or an explanation.
• Feedback example: “Your draft selects checking for paper registration. Its availability remains unknown. You can change or stop this plan.” Feedback describes explicit choices only; it cannot establish access, readiness, consent, skill or transfer.

PRIVACY AND ACCESS BOUNDARIES
Use no real passwords, identifiers, contact details, account information or service records. Concealment must explain that fictional notes remain in the current session. Clearing must remove candidate-held notes and visibly confirm removal; it must not promise removal of browser, operating-system or device records. Examine what appears after interruption and session ending. Wording, languages, keyboard access and other participation arrangements require review. Paper also needs readable wording, physical access and discreet handling.

COMPARISON AND OPEN QUESTIONS
Compare the candidate with the same paper card and an ordinary conversation with available staff. Review may favor either alternative or reject the workbench. Unknowns include actual route availability, assistance boundaries, staff capacity, language needs, shared-device behavior and whether another planning task is wanted. Preserve objections even if other reviewers favor the candidate.

EVIDENCE PLAN — ALL PENDING

Can they revise, conceal, clear and stop with understandable consequences? What reading, privacy and assistance burdens does each approach introduce? Use fictional content only and retain contrary accounts. Difficulty could reflect instructions or unavailable opportunities; apparent ease could reflect prior familiarity. Later transfer would need separate evidence in another fictional task and cannot be inferred from completion here.
