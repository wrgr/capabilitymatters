# Understand

Status and basis

This is the orchestrator’s working Understand artifact for a lifecycle rehearsal. It integrates the domain draft at sequence 4 and the Human-centered designer draft at sequence 6. These are provisional contributions, not approved requirements or participant evidence. The seed supplies the fictional investigation and constraints. The parent school-science iteration supplies background only. No technical checks, human approvals, classroom observations or learning outcomes have been supplied.

Problem and people

The proposed task is to help a fictional school garden team decide what its available records support. Learners choose a claim and an evidence-card pair, inspect recorded differences, encounter an additional record and decide how to represent their explanation afterward. The problem is an opportunity for evidence comparison and bounded explanation; a student reasoning deficit has not been established.

Intended users are middle-school students with varied reading and language access, science teachers and classroom support staff. Caregivers are affected-person reviewers. None are participants in this demonstration. A shared device makes participation and representation a group concern: operating the interface does not confer authority to speak for everyone.

Rival explanations for apparent difficulty include inaccessible language or unfamiliar units, confusing graphics or task navigation, time pressure, unequal device turns and reluctance to disagree with an adult. A learner may also understand the uncertainty while rejecting the framing. Neither completion nor fluent wording distinguishes these explanations. Understand therefore retains both interaction barriers and instructional framing as open explanations.

Source facts and interpretation boundaries

All observations belong to an invented 14-day story. A records water 1 story unit/day, bright light, final height 12 cm and upright leaves. B records water 2 story units/day, dim light, final height 18 cm and drooping leaves. C records water 2 story units/day, bright light, final height 10 cm and upright leaves. These are not measurements, plant-care advice or real outcomes.

Starting height, plant type and soil are unrecorded. Final height does not establish growth; leaf appearance does not establish health. A and B differ in both recorded water and light. A and C share recorded light and differ in recorded water, while unrecorded conditions remain unknown. B and C share recorded water and differ in recorded light. C challenges the universal descriptive claim that water-2 plants always finish taller in this story. It does not establish an opposite causal rule.

The seed requires two fixed fictional prepared comparisons for the test-planning bench but does not supply their complete records or choice mapping. Those examples are a Map dependency. They must be explicitly authored as additional invented examples, separately identified from A/B/C, and reviewed for consistency before Design or Build. They must not silently supply missing conditions for the original cards.

Human and system capability

Learners need opportunities to compare records, choose or supply a claim, select a pair, discuss uncertainty and reconsider without compulsory revision. Oral contributions, a scribe, another language, passing and dissent must remain possible. These supports depend on available people, time and materials; the interface cannot provide equitable participation by itself.

Teachers and support staff need manageable facilitation prompts and responsibility for language support, device turns and disputed attribution. Flexible roles such as evidence reader, operator and questioning voice may help, but rotation must not become a participation test. Any separate assessment remains a human responsibility outside this candidate; the tool must produce no automated grades, rankings, disability inference or proficiency judgment.

The system must make recorded conditions, fictional status and unknowns intelligible, preserve authored wording and display decision consequences descriptively. It must distinguish the initial record from subsequent proposals, avoid implied consensus and permit recovery from ordinary mistakes. It must support keyboard access, visible focus, readable responsive layout and text equivalents without network calls, storage, telemetry or external assets.
