# Design

## Problem and people

Conversation Lab proposes voluntary practice for first-time team leads using an invented missed-handoff story: a handoff note was absent on Monday, the next shift repeated a check, and the lead has not heard the colleague’s account. The task is to distinguish observation from explanation, choose an opening, reconsider changed information and prepare a feasible conversation proposal. The absent note establishes neither carelessness nor responsibility for the repeated check; their causal relationship remains unknown.

New leads, colleagues, managers, learning teams and employee representatives are prospective affected groups. None have participated. This is a revised rehearsal Design brief, not approved requirements or demonstrated proficiency. The previous workforce journal remains an immutable parent, not a measured baseline or approved intervention.

Sequence 15 integrates the specialist drafts at sequences 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13. Sequence 2 preserves parent material, including a hypothetical shared-workstation interruption. Sequence 18 introduces another hypothetical event: after a template-dependent proposal exists, an authorized fictional manager offers restored access on the condition that the colleague completes the note after their shift; coverage and protected time have been withdrawn. The fictional colleague refuses the additional work. Ownership remains unresolved. Neither hypothetical event is an observation or prediction.

Rival explanations include changed schedules, inaccessible materials, unclear ownership, inadequate coverage and conflicting expectations. Conversation difficulty may be secondary to those conditions. Training is not the automatic response.

## Human and system capability

Human requirements: participants need room to question observations, retain tentative interpretations, reject feedback, correct wording, withhold agreement, choose written participation, pass or stop without justification. Leads must distinguish inquiry from decisions requiring another person’s authority. A colleague’s refusal does not become a capability judgment. Managers must inspect protected time, coverage, access, ownership and suitable space. Authority to restore access does not establish authority to resolve every workflow condition or another person’s agreement. Facilitators need covered time and accessible materials; they must not grade speech, speed, confidence, eye contact or script fidelity. No resources, support owners or participation protections are secured.

System requirements: use deterministic choices and explicitly authored fictional examples. Separate source facts, possible explanations, missing accounts, initial history, current proposals and pending corrections. Represent access, relevant authority, protected capacity and agreement independently. Protected capacity comprises protected time and coverage; if either is unavailable, the combined condition is unavailable, and an unresolved component remains unknown. Do not use a single available-support label as a feasibility certificate.

Use fictional inputs only. Exclude real employee records, identifiable recordings, profiling, employee ranking, consequential HR advice, automatic scores, telemetry, storage and network requests. Keep only volatile state in the candidate frame; refresh or closure loses work. No export, sharing controls, certificates, attendance logs or aid-use records are proposed. Do not evaluate optional text or infer consent, readiness, reactions or learning needs. Humans retain consequential workplace decisions and any separate disclosure decision.

Language needs, reading demands, devices, assistive technology, suitable space and workflow resources are unknown. Responsive layout, plain wording, untimed keyboard use and visible focus are intended qualities requiring inspection. Recording nothing cannot prevent informal observation or employment pressure.
