Learning problem to investigate
The proposed capability map should distinguish knowing a conversation approach, using it in fictional practice, adapting it under workplace constraints and achieving an outcome that both people regard as respectful and useful. These are different claims. Course completion establishes neither readiness nor transfer. A polished rehearsal alone cannot establish workplace effectiveness, and a difficult workplace outcome does not by itself establish a capability deficit.

Discovery should explore three questions through fictional or generalized situations: What does the lead need to notice and decide? What should the colleague be able to question, clarify or change? What conditions make a useful conversation possible? Invite voluntary perspectives from leads, colleagues, managers, learning teams and employee representatives using the accessible choices proposed in sequence 6. Do not solicit recordings or identifiable accounts. Preserve conflicting answers rather than turning them into a common standard prematurely.

Provisional mapping template
For each fictional situation, record: the intended benefit for both people; decisions and adaptations that might require practice; competing workplace explanations; access and coverage conditions; unresolved dissent; and evidence needed to distinguish the hypotheses. Keep entries about situations and supports, not named employees.

Example hypothesis: a lead struggles to open a conversation when the concern is ambiguous. A possible learning need is distinguishing observations from interpretations and inviting correction. Rival explanations include unclear expectations, insufficient time to check the concern or fear of contradicting a manager. A next discovery step is to ask whether a fictional example presents a learning difficulty, a workplace barrier or both. No response or result is assumed.

Candidate learning approach
If discovery supports a learning need, consider short, protected practice sessions with participant-chosen fictional scenarios. Candidate supports include an optional example, prompts that can be reduced as desired, another attempt after feedback and a later variation requiring adaptation. These are design hypotheses to review, not a validated prescription. Participants should be able to pause, choose a written format, reject a scenario and challenge feedback. Agree what feedback concerns before practice; do not equate speed, eye contact, confidence or script fidelity with competence.

Evidence boundaries
A later evidence plan should distinguish participant experience, behavior in fictional practice and voluntary accounts of workplace use. Each answers a different question. Self-reported use may inform improvement but cannot alone establish effective transfer or causal benefit. Consider rival explanations such as changed workload, stronger managerial support, selective participation or familiarity with the practice task. Missing evidence must remain unknown, never a deficit. Evidence-sharing choices should be separate from access to learning, with audiences, retention and withdrawal limits established through human review.

Human and system responsibilities
Humans must judge acceptable outcomes, participation conditions, facilitation quality and whether staffing or workflow changes should precede training. Any system should support accessible practice and private reflection while keeping hypotheses, barriers and uncertainty visible. It cannot certify freely chosen participation or determine readiness from activity traces.

Non-AI option and next action
Retain facilitated peer practice with printed fictional scenarios, accessible written alternatives and private reflection. This option also needs covered time and human review. Next, incorporate the distinctions above into the provisional discovery protocol and mapping template, then seek affected-person review before field discovery. Preserve dissent over whether a map is needed at all. Modeling should await an explicit account of which needs remain hypotheses and what evidence is still absent.
