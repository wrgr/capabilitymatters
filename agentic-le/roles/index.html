Agentic learning engineering · Team roles

Who does what?

A learning engineer orchestrates eight complementary roles. Each profile contains a remit, inherited responsibilities, and requested LENS competency targets. Domain specializations add the context of a particular problem.

Responsibilities every role inherits

  • Distinguish supplied facts, assumptions, hypothetical events and missing evidence.
  • State human and system capability requirements; examine agency, dignity, access and workload.

Specialists brief the learning engineer. The learning engineer integrates their work; the scenario agent tests assumptions. Human approval remains a separate, accountable decision.

Inside each role

Orchestration

Learning engineer

Integrate learning sciences, human-centered design, systems requirements and evidence. Preserve dissent and choose the earliest stage to revisit.

Expected contribution: A stage artifact and decision that connect specialist drafts, preserve disagreements, and explain what to revisit. At Build, an executable candidate.

Meeting voice: Warm, decisive integrator. Brings the room together, makes disagreements visible, and ends with a concrete next step. This personality belongs to the conversational presentation, separate from the role contract.

What a contribution contains

The runner asks agents to expose their reasoning for review: a message; advantages, drawbacks, tradeoffs, risks and objections; separate human and system capability requirements; and implications for flourishing. A response also contains a draft artifact, a decision, any challenged assumption or stage to revisit, and references to earlier journal events. Fields may be empty when they do not apply.

Explore meeting conversation mode for source-linked highlights with distinct role voices. The original recorded turns remain available alongside the adaptation.

Only the learning engineer’s integrated response becomes a versioned stage artifact. The Build response contains executable candidate code. Scenario challenges are explicitly hypothetical; the transcript preserves them as challenges to requirements.

Requested proficiency and the evidence needed

These are proposed simulation expectations, not an adopted credentialing standard or an assessment of human or model proficiency. The five domain names come from Capability Matters’ public LENS framework.

Awareness

Identify the relevant issue, uncertainty and need for specialist or human review.

Evidence needed to review performance:

  • Name a constraint in context
  • Identify the limit of the available evidence

Applied contribution

Produce a bounded task artifact, compare explanations and revise it against a counterexample.

Evidence needed to review performance:

  • A task artifact with a stated criterion
  • Traceable supporting and conflicting evidence
  • Revision after a changed condition

Integrative orchestration

Integrate specialist work, preserve dissent, reconcile human/system requirements and choose an accountable next action.

Evidence needed to review performance:

  • A decision linking specialist contributions
  • Explicit tradeoffs and unresolved dissent
  • A justified return to the earliest affected stage

Inheritance supplies shared duties. Child targets replace the parent target for the same domain; other targets remain. It supplies no performance evidence. Review context-specific task performance, support conditions, evidence coverage and transfer before making a capability claim.

Reuse and extend the roles

Registry 1.1.0; framework 1.0.0. Extend an existing concrete profile in the versioned registry, name the domain assumptions and required practitioner validation, then test resolution. New runs store the exact resolved profiles and framework hashes. Historical runs retain their original contracts.

A future authoritative competency framework needs source and version identifiers, an explicit crosswalk and separately reviewed proficiency evidence. A name match or successful model response does not establish equivalence. Runtime permissions and human approval boundaries do not expand through role inheritance.
