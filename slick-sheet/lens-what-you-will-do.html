What you'll actually do

The question that comes up in every information session: not what LENS believes, but what a student does. This is the work — one capability problem carried end to end, and the artifact you produce at each step.

LENS is not another way to build a course. The unit of work is the system, and the deliverable is evidence.

You still design. But you design against a capability requirement decomposed from what the system has to deliver, you instrument what you build, and you report what the evidence forced you to change. The first move of the work is deciding whether a learning intervention is the right answer at all.

Every step is graded work

These are the actual assignments, not a metaphor for them. LEN 1 alone asks for a capability analysis, a documented iteration cycle, a coupling-failure analysis, and a recorded briefing.

Your domain is the material

Assignments run on your own professional setting wherever they can. Prior expertise is treated as an asset to work from, not a gap to remediate.

The capstone is the whole arc

LEN 10 runs steps 1 through 8 at a partner organization on a real capability problem, with at least one complete iteration cycle documented.

Coming across from LXD or AILE

If you come from learning experience design

Carries over The learning sciences, needs analysis, and design craft. LENS assumes them — the Learning Sciences Studio is a shared foundation course, and production of content and media stays with LXD.

What's new The unit of analysis moves outward: from the experience to the operational system that hosts it. You write capability requirements decomposed from a system requirement, and you measure against operational outcomes rather than completion and satisfaction.

The move you'll learn Telling a learning problem from a system problem — and being willing to report that the intervention you were asked for is not the fix.

If you come from AI leadership in education

Carries over Fluency with the tools, and the governance, adoption, and organizational questions that come with them. LENS treats AI as one case of human-system collaboration, not a separate subject.

What's new You specify the partnership as an engineered object: role architecture, authority gradients, mode and state transparency, recoverability — and delegation with revocation, where you name in advance the evidence that would take the delegation back.

The move you'll learn Measuring the human-machine team as a unit of analysis, and specifying capability requirements for systems that have not been fielded yet, from design artifacts alone.

What changes between arriving and leaving

You arrive able toYou leave able to
Attribute a performance problem to training by default. Diagnose whether the gap is rooted in learning, in system design, or in organizational conditions — before proposing anything.
Interpret simple descriptive data about learners — completion, scores — as someone else presents it. Instrument a learning environment ethically and produce decision-grade evidence: dashboards and reproducible reports linking learning measures to operational performance.
Recognize that learning solutions sit inside larger organizational and technical systems. Model the learning environment and its host system, translating capability requirements across the human-machine interface into design requirements.
Identify stakeholders in a familiar setting and describe their needs informally. Run a stakeholder and context analysis in a high-consequence environment using a human systems integration framework, producing a documented context model.
Use AI tools as a consumer. Design capability development for human-AI teams and specify learning requirements for systems not yet fielded.
Work inside your own professional silo, handing off at its boundary. Lead and integrate a learning engineering team across disciplines, with the shared vocabulary the work requires.

Where the value lands

  • Money not spent on the wrong fix

    Training is the default answer to a performance problem, and often the wrong one. A graduate who can attribute the gap — with evidence — stops the spend that was never going to work.

  • Evidence a decision-maker can act on

    Not completion rates. Dashboards, reproducible reports, and governance documentation that state what is known, what is assumed, and what would change the decision.

  • Capability specified before it is late

    Requirements written from design artifacts, before a system is fielded — so capability is designed into the lifecycle rather than bolted on when training arrives last.

Asked in every session

  • Do I need to code?
    No. Computational and AI methods are a recommended elective, not a gate. The required methods courses teach you to produce and appraise evidence, and to brief the specialist where depth is needed.
  • Do I have to work in healthcare or defense?
    No. Those are two of seven representative domains, and the list is not exhaustive. Education is on it as a domain and is also the discipline LENS works from.
  • Is this a research degree?
    No. Study design and human-subjects depth are treated at awareness and routed to an elective. The capstone is applied work in a real organization.
