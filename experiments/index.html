Experiments: labs and projects to explore

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A directory of local teaching labs, research prototypes and creative demonstrations, with links to projects hosted elsewhere.

How should I use it?

  1. Choose a category and open a resource. Start with its About this resource card and try one small comparison.
  2. For an externally hosted project, use its own instructions and review its stated scope before entering data.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

Compare what each resource makes observable: a mechanism, a decision, a design or a learning claim. LENS asks which capability and conditions are relevant and what additional evidence would establish transfer or benefit.

Products and case replays

Agentic AI Lab

capabilitymatters.org/agentic-le

Try candidate products and replay how a learning engineer orchestrates specialist agents through fictional workforce, school, and library projects. Inspect discussions, scenario challenges, revisions, and versioned artifacts; these are real agent runs on fictional problems, with human pilot approval and field evidence still pending.

Manager simulation

Andon Operations Lab

capabilitymatters.org/andon

Lead a fictional distribution shift: monitor queues, allocate help, contain abnormal work, support operators, and test changes against field evidence. Audit delivery, quality, responsiveness, and waiting separately, then replay the same conditions; model parameters are explicit teaching assumptions.

Teaching simulation

The Capability Pipeline

capabilitymatters.org/capability-pipeline

Explore seven numbered steps, with continuous refinement, through five worked examples; a companion branching simulation preserves its original refinement phase. Make decisions, inspect consequences, revise your strategy, and compare branches; the fictional scenarios use explicit teaching assumptions.

Interactive lab

MicroGPT for AI leaders

capabilitymatters.org/microgpt

A guided companion to Andrej Karpathy's microgpt, connecting model mechanics to perspectives from Andrew Ng and Dario Amodei. Train a tiny transformer in your browser, compare checkpoints, and expose how biased engagement labels can produce misleading scores. Explanations connect each result to the running code.

LLM101

capabilitymatters.org/llm101

Next-token prediction explained at five depths, so a reader can take the level they can actually use rather than the one the author found interesting. The build is written up beside it as a worked example of designing for a capability instead of for coverage.

Assessment prototype

Calibrated Judgment

calibratedjudgment.org

Grades an argumentative essay twice — once from the finished text, once from the transcript of the AI dialogue behind it — and reports the gap between them. Every score is anchored to a verbatim quote, and criteria a model should not settle alone route to the instructor, whose override becomes calibration data.

Read the learning guide

Mentoring prototype

ExpertTrace

experttrace.org

An AI mentor working from an operational knowledge corpus — symptom to fault to corrective action, plus the tacit cues experts never write down — that probes a learner through a live scenario at a chosen level of expertise. It assesses on unfamiliar scenarios, because repeating a known pattern is not transfer.

Read the learning guide

Training and mentorship

NeuroTrailblazers

neurotrailblazers.org

Training and mentorship for nanoscale connectomics: ten technical units from electron-microscopy basics through NeuroAI analysis, a curated journal club, and hands-on work against real datasets including MICrONS and FlyWire. Built for three audiences at once — students, the mentors supporting them, and programs adopting the model.

Browser game

Rainbow Bug Dash

capabilitymatters.org/rainbow-bug

Collect rainbows, dodge bugs, and explore a browser game co-created by Julian, age 6, with artificial intelligence (AI) as a creative partner. Pair the game with the Vibe Coding job aid by James Diamond and Will Gray-Roncal to try the same process: describe an idea, play with the result, and use your judgment to guide the next revision.

Physics sandbox

POP

wrgr.github.io/pop

A soap-bubble physics sandbox: shape a wand, watch the film billow and deform in moving air, then run the whole thing backwards — fit an ellipse to a photo of a real bubble and read the wind speed back out of the shape it left.
