# Product handoff and release

The lifecycle ends in a runnable candidate product. The current fictional feedback-practice product is at `/lifecycle-lab/product/`; its portable archive is `/lifecycle-lab/product/product.zip`. Replay remains at `/lifecycle-lab/replay/`.

The archive contains `candidate.html`, `requirements.md`, `artifacts.json`, `project-journal.json`, `manifest.json` and a README. The manifest links the product to the exact journal head and accepted Build content hash, records the Build revision and hashes each payload file. It preserves any recorded technical-verification event references. Legacy roles remain labeled legacy; proficiency is not assigned retroactively.

Product packaging rejects a missing executable Build. A complete local run automatically writes its handoff under the run's `product/` directory. Partial cycles may legitimately end without a product. Neither packaging nor the presence of HTML establishes that the product solves the intended problem.

Release states stay distinct:

- **Candidate:** runnable and inspectable; its requirements and evidence travel with it.
- **Reviewed pilot:** accountable people approve the scope, affected-person participation, accessible operation, data handling, comparison and stopping rules. The current exemplar has not reached this state.
- **Operational release:** evidence and operational responsibilities justify the stated use. Reliability, correction, maintenance, workload and the ability to stop remain necessary.

Do not promote release state automatically from stage completion, model agreement, technical tests or requested proficiency. Keep a non-AI alternative and organizational repair in scope. Evaluate effects on agency, dignity, access, belonging, workload and learning with affected people; the candidate has no demonstrated flourishing outcome.
