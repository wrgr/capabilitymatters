# Requested and demonstrated proficiency

`src/data/lifecycle/competencies.json` carries the existing public LENS domains: Systems Analysis, Iterative Development, Human-System Collaboration, Test & Evaluation, and Sociotechnical Constraints. `src/lib/lens.ts` reads those names so the case pages, role explorer and runner share a canonical list.

The simulation's contribution descriptors are a proposed project contract. They are not an adopted LENS attainment scale, an IEEE standard or a human credential. Authoritative competencies and attainment descriptors can be integrated later with explicit source/version records and a reviewed mapping.

| Requested level | Work expected in the simulation | Evidence needed before a performance claim |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Identify a relevant issue, uncertainty and need for specialist review | Context-specific identification of a constraint and an evidence limit |
| Applied contribution | Produce a bounded artifact, compare explanations, revise against a counterexample | A task artifact, stated criterion, supporting/conflicting evidence and a reviewable revision |
| Integrative orchestration | Integrate disciplinary work, preserve dissent, reconcile human/system tradeoffs and choose an accountable next action | Linked specialist contributions, explicit unresolved issues and a justified lifecycle decision |

An inherited target says what to ask the role to contribute. It supplies no evidence that an agent or person performs at that level. Every resolved role therefore records demonstrated proficiency as unverified. A fluent response, completed activity, model agreement or passed syntax check cannot promote that status.

Future evidence records should identify the subject (person, team, agent configuration or human-system arrangement), competency and framework version, representative task, conditions and support, performance criterion, inspected artifact, reviewer, decision, coverage and limits. Distinguish immediate performance, retained learning, transfer and operational effects. Include counterevidence and contested interpretations. Missing evidence is unknown; do not convert it to zero or a deficit.

Role inheritance and proficiency evidence remain separate data. A reviewed evidence record may support a bounded claim about a particular configuration and task. It cannot silently transfer to another model, changed tool access, a different workplace or a human participant. Human approval and consequential decisions remain outside simulated proficiency targets.
