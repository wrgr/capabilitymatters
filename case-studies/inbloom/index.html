inBloom

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A documented case about inBloom. The roughly $100 million shared student-data initiative wound down in April 2014 after partner withdrawals and New York’s new restrictions on state sharing of student data.

How should I use it?

  1. Read the narrative and five beats, keeping the disclosures and scope limits in view.
  2. Compare the LE insight with the LENS approach. Identify the system condition that changed what people could do, then check the cited evidence before applying the lesson elsewhere.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

inBloom proposed combining student records so teachers could use information from multiple applications. Families and teachers asked who would see the records, what vendors could do with them and how a family could object. The company argued that its protections were stronger than existing systems. New York restricted state sharing and inBloom closed in April 2014. The record shows why a classroom data pilot needs to test both whether teachers can act on the information and whether families have meaningful recourse.

In brief

inBloom proposed a shared data infrastructure for K–12 student records, backed by the Gates Foundation and Carnegie Corporation of New York. Parents and teachers raised concerns about sensitive data, vendors, access and oversight. As partners withdrew, New York’s 2014 budget restricted state sharing with data-dashboard infrastructure and the nonprofit announced it would wind down. The CEO argued that the system offered stronger protection than existing arrangements and blamed public misunderstanding. Parent and teacher advocates disputed the balance of benefit and risk. The record documents a governance breakdown. Whether the platform would have improved instruction at its intended scale remains open.

The case in five beats

  1. The proposal linked student records and classroom applications
  2. Families and teachers questioned the benefits and risks of data sharing
  3. Partners withdrew and New York restricted state sharing
  4. The CEO and parent advocates differed on privacy protections and acceptable risk
  5. An instructional pilot would need to test both classroom use and meaningful recourse

What this case does not show

Closure does not establish technical success or failure at intended scale. The record of opposition and withdrawal does not isolate one cause.
