Crew Resource Management & CAST

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A documented case about Crew Resource Management & CAST. CRM formalized team coordination and recurrent practice. The FAA reports an 83% fall in U.S. commercial aviation fatality risk over 1998–2008 across a portfolio of safety changes.

How should I use it?

  1. Read the narrative and five beats, keeping the disclosures and scope limits in view.
  2. Compare the LE insight with the LENS approach. Identify the system condition that changed what people could do, then check the cited evidence before applying the lesson elsewhere.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

CRM defines and rehearses observable crew behaviors. CAST supplies a separate industry process for identifying hazards and evaluating a portfolio of responses. Team performance and industry safety outcomes need measures suited to their different scales. Measure whether a crew shares information, cross-checks and escalates concerns during an unfamiliar simulator exercise. Follow up with operational evidence about whether the practice transfers. Use CAST to study how the industry finds hazards and monitors uptake of responses.

Crew Resource Management made cockpit coordination something a team could practice and demonstrate. Crews learned how to share information, challenge a decision and manage workload together. The Commercial Aviation Safety Team, a separate industry effort, used operating data to identify hazards and track safety enhancements.

The 83% decline in U.S. commercial aviation fatality risk between 1998 and 2008 is a portfolio figure. The FAA credits CAST’s work alongside new aircraft, regulations and other activities. To judge what CRM contributes, start closer to its intended effect and assess crew behaviors in exercises and line operations.

In brief

The 1977 Tenerife collision drew attention to cockpit coordination, communication and authority. Crew Resource Management (CRM), introduced by United Airlines in 1981, gave crews ways to practice briefings, cross-checking and speaking up. Later FAA guidance called for reinforcement and assessment in operationally relevant exercises. The separate Commercial Aviation Safety Team (CAST) analyzed hazards and tracked industry safety enhancements. The FAA credits CAST’s work alongside aircraft, regulatory and other changes for an 83% fall in fatality risk. That figure describes the portfolio and cannot isolate CRM’s contribution.

The case in five beats

  1. Tenerife drew attention to cockpit coordination, communication and authority
  2. United introduced CRM training with structured crew practices in 1981
  3. CRM made team communication and escalation observable and practicable
  4. CAST analyzed aviation hazards and tracked the safety portfolio
  5. The portfolio trend cannot isolate CRM’s own effect
