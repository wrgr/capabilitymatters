Keystone ICU / Pronovost Checklist

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A documented case about Keystone ICU / Pronovost Checklist. Central-line-associated bloodstream infections (CLABSI) reduced to near zero across 103 Michigan ICUs; ~1,500 lives saved in 18 months; ~$175M saved; sustained at ten years

How should I use it?

  1. Read the narrative and five beats, keeping the disclosures and scope limits in view.
  2. Compare the LE insight with the LENS approach. Identify the system condition that changed what people could do, then check the cited evidence before applying the lesson elsewhere.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

Keystone illustrates why an apparently simple checklist should be examined as one part of a larger intervention involving clinical practice, team roles, supplies, organizational support, and feedback. Infection rates declined after implementation of the multi-component program, but the study did not isolate the effects of individual components or establish that any particular pair was necessary or sufficient. For learning engineering, the useful question is which mechanisms plausibly contributed, what the evidence actually supports, and what would need to be tested in a new setting. This case can be used to practice learning-engineering analysis: define the clinical capability of interest, map the people, tools, responsibilities, and work conditions involved, and distinguish observed outcomes from proposed causal mechanisms. As an illustrative student exercise, identify the evidence that would help assess implementation, equity, transfer, and sustainability. This is an example of how to use the case, not a statement of LENS course requirements or assigned studio work.

Peter Pronovost’s central-line checklist has five items, and not one of them was news to the physicians it governed. The challenge included reliable adherence to established practices and whether staff could speak up when steps were missed. Keystone paired the checklist with something harder to engineer than a list: nurses were empowered to stop a non-emergency procedure whenever a step was skipped. Across 103 Michigan ICUs, reported bloodstream infection rates fell sharply; follow-up studies examined sustained reductions. One plausible mechanism was permission to intervene, built into the work system. The honest limit is that the authority travelled inside a bundle — education, a dedicated line cart, daily catheter-review goals, monthly rate feedback — that the trial tested whole and cannot decompose.

In brief

Peter Pronovost's central-line checklist has five items — wash hands, use full barrier precautions, clean the skin with chlorhexidine, avoid the femoral insertion site, and remove unnecessary catheters — describing established infection-prevention practices. The project addressed reliable implementation of those practices rather than simply conveying new knowledge. The Keystone project, launched across 103 Michigan ICUs in 2004, paired the checklist with a cultural change: nurses were empowered to stop the procedure, in non- emergency situations, whenever a step was skipped. That authorization is a plausible contributing mechanism, but it was part of a multi-component bundle (clinician education, a dedicated line cart, daily catheter-review goals, and monthly infection-rate feedback) that the trial tested as a whole and could not decompose. Central-line-associated bloodstream infections fell to near zero, an estimated 1,500 lives and $175 million were saved in eighteen months, and the effect was sustained at ten years.

The case in five beats

  1. The project targeted persistent central-line infections through changes to sterile practice, workflow, and safety culture
  2. The intervention included a five-item checklist, nurse stop authority, education, supplies, and feedback
  3. The authority to stop a non-emergency procedure addressed a potential barrier across clinical hierarchies
  4. CLABSI rates fell near zero across 103 ICUs; effect sustained at ten years
  5. Related safety-program approaches were disseminated more widely; implementation and outcomes vary by setting
