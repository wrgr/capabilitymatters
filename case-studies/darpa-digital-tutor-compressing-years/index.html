DARPA Digital Tutor

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

How should I use it?

  1. Read the narrative and five beats, keeping the disclosures and scope limits in view.

DARPA built the Digital Tutor by studying expert tutors and modeling their practice in software. An Institute for Defense Analyses assessment for the sponsor found that graduates outscored selected experienced Navy technicians on an individual knowledge test after 16 weeks. The comparison used twelve people per group, without random assignment. Team exercises captured a different outcome, and the program’s overall design rating did not significantly exceed the fleet comparison.

This is a case in designing instruction around work people must perform and measuring different parts of capability separately. To test whether the results carry into fleet work, follow graduates into assignments and assess what they can diagnose and repair over time.

In brief

DARPA designed a digital tutor based on expert one-on-one tutoring for Navy information systems technicians. The IDA assessment for the sponsor compared 12 graduates of the 16-week program with 12 standard-course graduates and 12 fleet technicians averaging 9.1 years of experience. The groups were selected rather than randomly assigned. The digital tutor group outscored the fleet comparison group on the individual knowledge test, with an effect size of 4.30. Practical exercises were done in teams. Results varied by task, and the overall design rating was not significantly higher than the fleet group’s. The assessment did not measure sustained fleet performance.

The case in five beats

  1. The Digital Tutor modeled expert one-on-one teaching for Navy IT tasks
  2. The sponsor’s assessment compared three nonrandom groups of twelve
  3. The knowledge-test effect versus fleet technicians was 4.30
  4. Practical exercises were done in teams and results varied
  5. The assessment did not establish later on-the-job transfer
