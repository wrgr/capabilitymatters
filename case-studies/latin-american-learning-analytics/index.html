Latin American Learning Analytics Project

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A documented case about Latin American Learning Analytics Project. The LALA consortium used evidence from four universities in Chile and Ecuador to inform an adoption framework. Project records document pilots in 2019–2020.

How should I use it?

  1. Read the narrative and five beats, keeping the disclosures and scope limits in view.
  2. Compare the LE insight with the LENS approach. Identify the system condition that changed what people could do, then check the cited evidence before applying the lesson elsewhere.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

A risk signal can support learning only if advisers and students can interpret it and act on it. Local inquiry helps reveal where the tool, policy, or workflow needs to change. Understand what students need from advising and map the route from a risk signal to support. Involve students and advisers in designing a prototype. In a pilot, observe whether advisers can explain a signal and arrange timely support, and whether students can question a mistaken flag. Refine the tool and the rules for using it based on those observations.

An adviser who sees that a student has been flagged still needs to understand why and decide what support is available. LALA began by asking students, staff, and university leaders what those decisions required in their own institutions. The pilots then gave local teams a way to try adapted tools in the work they were meant to support.

In brief

The Latin American Learning Analytics project adapted European research instruments to study local needs. Researchers used interviews, focus groups, and surveys to hear from managers, teaching staff, and students. The consortium developed a framework covering institutional, technical, ethical, and community concerns and reported pilots of locally adapted advising and early-warning tools.

The case in five beats

  1. The team began by investigating how universities and students would use analytics.
  2. Stakeholders identified needs for timely support and useful feedback.
  3. The framework connected technical design with institutional and ethical decisions.
  4. Universities adapted tools to local workflows and reported pilot experience.
  5. Long-term student outcomes require separate evaluation.

What this case does not show

The needs study and project records document design and pilot implementation. They do not establish long-term gains in retention or achievement.
