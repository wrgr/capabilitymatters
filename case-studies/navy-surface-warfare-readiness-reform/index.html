Navy Surface Warfare Readiness Reform

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A documented case about Navy Surface Warfare Readiness Reform. The Navy reported that ten career assessments and checks were in place by 2022. GAO closed all four recommendations as implemented after follow-up through 2024.

How should I use it?

  1. Read the narrative and five beats, keeping the disclosures and scope limits in view.
  2. Compare the LE insight with the LENS approach. Identify the system condition that changed what people could do, then check the cited evidence before applying the lesson elsewhere.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

A qualification system can show how performance develops across a career. Common scoring criteria, experience records, and feedback help teams decide where practice or assessment should change. Map what an officer must demonstrate in a simulator and during supervised work at sea. Design common scoring criteria and instrument both settings. Evaluate whether performance holds after a delay and under changed conditions. Use discrepancies between the two settings to refine practice and qualification.

The Navy’s reforms give readers a case about assessing whether training transfers into work. The evaluation question is what an officer can demonstrate in a simulator, what they can still do later at sea, and how the Navy can use differences between those observations to improve preparation.

In brief

After the 2017 destroyer collisions, the Navy expanded ship-driving practice and worked to standardize qualification. Initial classroom and simulator instruction had already resumed in 2010. In 2019 GAO found gaps in how the Navy planned to evaluate its changes. The follow-up documents career assessments, recurring fleet feedback, common qualification criteria, and analysis of experience logs alongside assessment results.

The case in five beats

  1. Classroom and simulator instruction had resumed before the 2017 collisions.
  2. The Navy expanded preparation and added career assessments.
  3. GAO identified gaps in the evaluation of the reforms.
  4. Follow-up documented common criteria, feedback, and analysis of experience logs.
  5. Operational transfer remains a separate question for evaluation.

What this case does not show

GAO's closure decisions document implementation of the recommendations. They do not establish the reforms' causal effect on proficiency or safety at sea.
