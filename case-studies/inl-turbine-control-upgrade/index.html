INL Turbine-Control Upgrade

About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A documented case about INL Turbine-Control Upgrade. Licensed operators evaluated turbine-control interfaces in a simulator. Their observations informed design changes before formal validation.

How should I use it?

  1. Read the narrative and five beats, keeping the disclosures and scope limits in view.
  2. Compare the LE insight with the LENS approach. Identify the system condition that changed what people could do, then check the cited evidence before applying the lesson elsewhere.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

An operator’s performance reflects the interface, procedures, and preparation together. Simulator evidence can help a team distinguish a confusing display from a task that needs more practice. Understand and map what operators must recognize and communicate. Design and build a prototype, then instrument representative simulator tasks with observations and debriefs. Experiment with interface and procedure changes and evaluate where crews still struggle. Refine the display and use the findings to specify practice, assessment, and evidence required before cutover.

Idaho National Laboratory and a utility brought operators, instructors, and designers together to see how a digital turbine-control prototype supported familiar work. The workshop gave the design team direct feedback before formal validation. For LENS, the next question is what evidence would justify cutover: what crews can reliably do with the revised interface and procedures, how practice changes that performance, and what still needs to be tested.

In brief

Idaho National Laboratory and a utility used workshops to refine a digital turbine-control interface. One crew of three experienced licensed operators worked with the existing interface and a dynamic prototype in a simulator. Observations and operator feedback helped the team identify usability problems and refine the design. A related conference paper described operator training and integrated validation as planned work for a paired upgrade.

The case in five beats

  1. A digital interface changes the information available to operators and how they use it.
  2. Operators, trainers, and engineers review the prototype together.
  3. The simulator workshop exposes usability issues under representative conditions.
  4. The team uses the observations to refine the interface.
  5. Training, formal validation, and field readiness require separate evidence.

What this case does not show

The workshop used one experienced crew. It provided design feedback before formal validation. Training gains, transfer to other plants, and safety after cutover would require separate evidence.
