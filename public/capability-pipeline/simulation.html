About this resource · How to use it · Learning engineering connections

What is this resource?

A branching fictional scenario lets you edit a strategy, inspect consequences and compare decisions. Probabilities, effect sizes and outcome ranges are authored teaching assumptions, not estimates of real-world impact. The adventure includes a final refinement decision; use evidence to revisit assumptions throughout.

How should I use it?

  1. Begin with Play a scenario: read the evidence and decision guidance, choose a strategy and commit it to reveal the outcome.
  2. Use Decisions & branches to compare a revised strategy. Separate consequences of a decision from variation in the assumed future.
  3. Use Edit the scenario to inspect assumptions. Export the run before clearing browser storage; written explanations are recorded without machine scoring.

How does this connect to LENS and learning engineering?

The exercise rehearses iteration, sensitivity analysis and evidence-based revision. LENS requires capability claims to include access, authority and usable opportunities to act; the simulation makes those constraints explicit. A favorable simulated result is a prompt for investigation, not evidence that a learning intervention works in practice.

Provenance, privacy, and limitations

No account, analytics, network requests, or model calls. Run data are stored in this browser when permitted. Export a copy before changing browsers or clearing storage. Do not enter identifiable student information.

Author controls are not access-controlled. This is a teaching and discussion tool, not a secure assessment system or a validated prediction model. Written reasoning is recorded but is not machine-scored.

Model rationale and evidence boundary

Feasibility, authority, access and observability are explicit constraints. Declining, pausing and changing leverage are decisions, not random failures. Outcome ranges are authored scenario assumptions, not empirically calibrated probabilities. The adverse/central/favorable choices are sensitivity tests; no probability is assigned to those futures. Learning impacts remain synthetic, and process metrics cannot establish causal benefit.

Methodological references: RAND: robust decisions under deep uncertainty; HM Treasury: handling complexity in evaluation. These support the approach, not the numerical settings.
