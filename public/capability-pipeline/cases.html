The iteration cycle, worked through five real cases

The Capability Pipeline

Eight steps, one loop: understand → map → design → build → instrument → deploy → evaluate → refine — where refine can send you all the way back to understand, not just back to adjusting what you built. The same eight questions, asked of a hospital screening workflow, a federal insurance marketplace, an airliner, a math-tutoring program at scale, and a self-driving car. What changes case to case isn't the shape of the loop — it's which single step the story actually turns on.

The diagram

Read it left to right the first time. After that, read it as a circle — the point of the eighth step is to send you back to the first.

The step that decides the story is rarely evaluate. It's usually earlier — design, build, or instrument — because those steps determine what evaluate is even able to see. A system instrumented to track only its own headline metric cannot evaluate a cost that metric doesn't measure.

Below, five cases — one apiece from government services, aviation, healthcare, education, and autonomous vehicles — are walked through all eight steps. Each carries one clearly marked point where a different upstream decision would plausibly have produced a different downstream result.

Learning guide. Open a step for why it matters, a decision cue and a question to apply to this case. Teaching commentary is an interpretation of the presented narrative, not new historical evidence. In Find the turn and Predict first, the decision-specific explanation appears only after your guess or reveal.

Pick a case

Same eight steps, five different systems. Each card names the domain, the real frame, and how the case actually turned out. Three ways to work a case are below the picker — study it worked out, find where it turns yourself, or commit to a call before you're shown what happened.

The three modes. Built 2026-09-23 around a 4C/ID-informed course design (whole-task practice with fading scaffolding; the worked-example effect for early study, shifting toward independent problem-solving; Kapur's productive failure for a short commit-before-reveal moment). Study is the worked example: everything shown, the turn marked. Find the turn removes the marker and asks students to locate and defend it themselves — the same case, a harder ask. Predict first stops the story right before the turn and asks for a real commitment before revealing what happened, so the prediction is made under genuine uncertainty rather than in hindsight. Which mode a given case uses in a given week is an assignment-design decision, not a property of the case — the same case can serve as this week's worked example and a later week's "find the turn" exercise.
