# Design

## Problem and people

Conversation Lab proposes voluntary practice for first-time team leads using an invented missed-handoff story: a handoff note was absent on Monday, the next shift repeated a check, and the lead has not heard the colleague’s account. The task is to distinguish observation from explanation, choose an opening, reconsider changed information and prepare a feasible conversation proposal. The absent note establishes neither carelessness nor responsibility for the repeated check; their causal relationship remains unknown.

New leads, colleagues, managers, learning teams and employee representatives are prospective affected groups. None have participated. This is a revised rehearsal Design brief, not approved requirements or demonstrated proficiency. The previous workforce journal remains an immutable parent, not a measured baseline or approved intervention.

Sequence 15 integrates the specialist drafts at sequences 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13. Sequence 2 preserves parent material, including a hypothetical shared-workstation interruption. Sequence 18 introduces another hypothetical event: after a template-dependent proposal exists, an authorized fictional manager offers restored access on the condition that the colleague completes the note after their shift; coverage and protected time have been withdrawn. The fictional colleague refuses the additional work. Ownership remains unresolved. Neither hypothetical event is an observation or prediction.

Rival explanations include changed schedules, inaccessible materials, unclear ownership, inadequate coverage and conflicting expectations. Conversation difficulty may be secondary to those conditions. Training is not the automatic response.

## Human and system capability

Human requirements: participants need room to question observations, retain tentative interpretations, reject feedback, correct wording, withhold agreement, choose written participation, pass or stop without justification. Leads must distinguish inquiry from decisions requiring another person’s authority. A colleague’s refusal does not become a capability judgment. Managers must inspect protected time, coverage, access, ownership and suitable space. Authority to restore access does not establish authority to resolve every workflow condition or another person’s agreement. Facilitators need covered time and accessible materials; they must not grade speech, speed, confidence, eye contact or script fidelity. No resources, support owners or participation protections are secured.

System requirements: use deterministic choices and explicitly authored fictional examples. Separate source facts, possible explanations, missing accounts, initial history, current proposals and pending corrections. Represent access, relevant authority, protected capacity and agreement independently. Protected capacity comprises protected time and coverage; if either is unavailable, the combined condition is unavailable, and an unresolved component remains unknown. Do not use a single available-support label as a feasibility certificate.

Use fictional inputs only. Exclude real employee records, identifiable recordings, profiling, employee ranking, consequential HR advice, automatic scores, telemetry, storage and network requests. Keep only volatile state in the candidate frame; refresh or closure loses work. No export, sharing controls, certificates, attendance logs or aid-use records are proposed. Do not evaluate optional text or infer consent, readiness, reactions or learning needs. Humans retain consequential workplace decisions and any separate disclosure decision.

Language needs, reading demands, devices, assistive technology, suitable space and workflow resources are unknown. Responsive layout, plain wording, untimed keyboard use and visible focus are intended qualities requiring inspection. Recording nothing cannot prevent informal observation or employment pressure.

## Experience and decisions

The main task produces one conversation proposal through brief choices rather than a long questionnaire. Begin with a linear situation board labelled Observed fictional facts, Possible explanations and Missing context. Show the absent note and repeated check as observations; explanations remain tentative, including when selected. Keep the unheard colleague account and uncertain causal relationship visible. Do not rely on color, dragging, hover or spatial position.

Offer exact opening previews: “I noticed Monday’s handoff note was absent. What was happening from your perspective?”; “Can we check how the handoff was meant to work on Monday?”; and “I need more context before deciding our next step.” Choose an opening and provisional next move without mandatory typing. Pass, Stop, Cover and Paper activity remain available without reasons. Explain session loss before entry; place detailed review metadata in Help.

Each opening changes an explicitly authored possible exchange and descriptive feedback. Include a colleague offering context, questioning an interpretation or asking to discuss it later. Label dialogue beside the preview as invented possibilities, not predicted reactions or evidence about people. Participants can reject examples and feedback, retain their proposal or retry an opening against another authored possibility. No branch receives praise, blame, scores or a preferred-answer marker.

Capture the Initial proposal before revealing the supplied context: the rota changed, the template was inaccessible to the colleague, and the expected owner was unclear. This adds information without proving a complete cause. Invite retaining or revising the interpretation and next move. Retaining wording must still display the new constraints.

For a next move involving template use or additional handoff work, reveal relevant dependency choices: access, authority to resolve the proposed condition, and protected time/coverage, each Available, Unavailable or Unknown within the fictional prospective situation. Show what authority is needed and what work remains unassigned. Offer checking expectations, requesting support or pausing. Unknown or unavailable conditions produce a conditional proposal, inquiry or pause; they never produce an unconditional commitment. Even available support leaves reciprocal agreement unresolved.

Introduce sequence 18’s changed-condition card after a template-dependent initial proposal exists. A pending correction may remain open when the participant activates the card so the recovery rule is inspectable. Display: “Access offered; protected capacity unavailable; colleague agreement withheld; ownership unresolved.” Display the authored response: “Making the template accessible does not give me time to complete it after my shift. I cannot agree to that extra work.” Label the event and response hypothetical. An offer to restore access is distinct from confirmed availability, and neither supplies time or agreement.

After that event, offer three concrete routes: request clarification of protected capacity and ownership; reconsider the next move; or pause. Authored synthesis must leave additional work unassigned and avoid promising after-shift completion. Retaining wording or rejecting feedback remains possible, with the conflict independently visible. Prospective support changes must not erase the recorded refusal or invent later agreement. They must also leave Monday’s inaccessible template, unclear owner and the withdrawal event intact as fictional history.

Show clearly labelled Initial proposal and Current proposal. Each contains observation, invitation, next move, relevant support, authority, protected capacity, unassigned work and unresolved questions or disagreement. State neutrally what changed or was retained. Put “Fictional practice proposal; no workplace agreement or capability judgment” beside the output. On narrow screens, versions may appear sequentially with explicit labels.

Feedback describes what wording states, whose account it invites and what remains unresolved. A carelessness interpretation remains an explanation rather than an observed cause. A process inquiry identifies the expectation being checked. Template use names access, ownership and capacity conditions. Pausing leaves a suitable opportunity and agreement unarranged. Feedback is contestable; revising is not itself success.

Use at most two optional main-path text fields: replacement wording and an unresolved question, both for fictional content only. A component selector identifies what the replacement changes. Review shows the exact replacement, affected component and dependency summary before Apply or Cancel. Cancel preserves the current proposal. Applied participant wording remains verbatim and labelled; the candidate cannot certify its feasibility. Independent conditions remain visible even if the text contradicts them.

A relevant context or support change, including sequence 18’s event, invalidates any pending correction review. Preserve the draft, mark the review stale and disable Apply until the participant explicitly reviews the exact replacement with the updated conditions. Renewed review enables a separate Apply decision; reviewing alone does not apply it. Cancel preserves current wording and the newly revealed conditions rather than reversing the event.

Cover immediately conceals all content surfaces, including scenario, history, current plans, text fields, pending reviews, dialogue, second-situation progress and open detail views. Concealed content must be unavailable through ordinary assistive-technology navigation. Move focus to a neutral screen. Resume is explicit, restores current corrections and the capacity conflict, and offers optional orientation without a recall task. Stale reviews remain stale. No automatic resumption, interruption logs or inactivity assessment.

Finish and clear for handoff is separate from Cover. Describe the scope and require confirmation; Cancel retains the session. Confirmation resets every candidate value and rendered surface, including both situations, initial/current plans, support and capacity choices, refusal state, drafts and pending previews, then shows a neutral start screen. Clearing must work from Cover without revealing content. Cover retains volatile work; clearing removes candidate session state. Neither prevents prior viewing or external capture.

Offer one optional second situation: “Two shifts each understood the other to own the final checklist check.” Begin with fewer suggestions and keep help immediately available without recording its use. Produce an ownership-focused opening and next move through choices. Keep its source data and state separate: do not inherit Monday’s access barrier or sequence 18’s refusal and capacity withdrawal. Call it supported practice, not a transfer test.

## Tradeoffs and alternatives

Choices reduce entry burden but constrain expression; optional correction and unresolved questions preserve another route. Examples support participation but can invite imitation; rejecting them and requesting help remain legitimate. Dependency details add work, so reveal them when the next move requires them. Before/after comparison makes revision inspectable without rewarding change.

Retain dissent that organizational repair or no practice is preferable. An accountable review of rota, coverage, protected time, ownership and accessible materials is a non-learning option. The candidate cannot authorize repairs. Deterministic browser practice and facilitated paper practice are non-AI options; neither secures participation protections.

Complete paper materials: a situation card containing the three initial story statements; a board labelled Observed facts, Possible explanations and Missing context; the three exact opening cards above; blank replacement, Pass and Stop cards. A reveal card states the changed rota, inaccessible template and unclear owner, labelled invented context rather than a complete cause. Authored response cards say “Here is additional context”, “I question that interpretation” and “I would prefer to discuss this later”, labelled possibilities rather than predictions.

Provide Initial proposal and Current proposal sheets with spaces for observation, invitation, next move, access, authority, protected time/coverage, agreement, unassigned work and unresolved questions. Supply Available, Unavailable and Unknown cards for support conditions; agreement remains separately unresolved or withheld. Add sequence 18’s complete changed-condition card and exact refusal above. Add a feedback card asking: “Which wording is observation or explanation?”, “Whose account or agreement is missing?” and “What support makes the move feasible?” Include a blank cover sheet and the exact second-situation card, with opening suggestions available on request.

Facilitation: arrange voluntary participation, protected time, accessible materials, suitable space and participant-controlled disposal first. Present the situation, invite an opening or Pass, and record the initial proposal. Release the reveal; for a template-dependent proposal, release the changed-condition card after the initial plan exists. Preserve refusal and leave additional work unassigned. Invite retention, one-component revision or stopping; describe implications without grading. Show the exact replacement and conditions before accepting it; cancellation preserves the current sheet. If conditions change during review, review again before acceptance. Offer an optional response-card retry or the separate second situation. Written selection is sufficient; speech and role-play are optional. Cover for interruption and resume only by participant choice. Do not solicit personal accounts, collect or photograph sheets, grade aid use, record attendance for assessment or require sharing. Dispose under the participant-controlled arrangement. These conditions are not secured.
