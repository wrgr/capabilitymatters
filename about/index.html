A question worth asking

What should people be able to do—and under what conditions?

Define the capability. Examine the work system. Try a small change. Look for evidence of performance, learning, and transfer.

A polished output or a completed activity is a starting point for inquiry, not proof of capability.

Context, people & evidence

Read with the evidence in view.

Case studies document examples and their limitations. Labs and prototypes let you examine a mechanism or rehearse a decision; availability does not establish effectiveness. Capability Matters is a LENS community paper on capability, coupled learner and system performance, and human flourishing.

For the wider field’s reading lists, tools, events, and community, visit the Learning Engineering Commons and IEEE ICICLE.

Where it applies

LENS focuses on high-consequence, high-impact domains — where human capability is inseparable from system performance and the cost of getting it wrong is measured in lives, readiness, and equity. Seven domains recur across the program's case material and teaching: healthcare, defense & national security, education at scale, aviation, energy, manufacturing & industrial, and government & public infrastructure.

The LENS framework

LENS reads every case through five competencies. Each case study on this site is tagged to the competencies it exercises, so the same framework runs from a hospital to a flight deck to a public-sector IT migration:

  1. Systems Analysis

    Represent people, tools, requirements, interfaces and feedback to locate a capability gap in its operating system.

  2. Iterative Development

    Frame a problem, test a bounded change, examine the evidence and revisit the next decision. Refine throughout the cycle.

  3. Human-System Collaboration

    Design roles, authority, coordination and opportunities for people to recognize, question and intervene in system behavior.

  4. Data, Measurement and Evaluation

    Choose observations and instruments that can test a capability claim, and explain uncertainty and limits on the inference.

  5. Navigating Sociotechnical Constraints

    Account for institutional rules, incentives, resources, access and governance when deciding what can be built and sustained.
