LENS · for prospective students

What you'll actually do

The question that comes up in every information session isn't what LENS believes. It's what a student does. This is the work: one capability problem carried end to end, the artifact you produce at each step, and what changes for you if you're arriving from learning experience design or from AI leadership in education.

LENS is not another way to build a course. The unit of work is the system, and the deliverable is evidence.

You still design. But you design against a capability requirement decomposed from what the system has to deliver, you instrument what you build, and you report what the evidence forced you to change. The first move of the work is deciding whether a learning intervention is the right answer at all.

One problem, end to end

Eight steps, in the order you work them. The right-hand column is what you hand in — these are the actual assignments, not a metaphor for them.

  1. 1

    Frame it

    Sit with the people doing the work. Separate the complaint from the failure, and restate the request as a system performance requirement rather than a training order.

    Problem framing with stakeholder and context analysis LEN 6

  2. 2

    Map capabilities and conditions

    Diagram the learning environment and the operational system that hosts it — components, interfaces, feedback loops — and place the capability decisions on the system's lifecycle.

    Capability analysis and system model LEN 1 · Assignment 1

  3. 3

    Attribute the gap

    Decide whether the shortfall sits in what people can do, in how the system is designed, or in how the organization runs — and name the evidence that would change your answer.

    Gap attribution with its evidence basis LEN 4

  4. 4

    Design it

    Build the smallest thing that could work — including the human-machine boundary: who holds which role, what the system decides, and what evidence takes a delegation back.

    Role architecture and delegation-with-revocation spec LEN 2

  5. 5

    Instrument it

    Measure ethically, at the speed the operation actually moves, and measure the team as a unit rather than only the individual operator. State what you cannot see.

    Instrumentation plan and evidence dashboard LEN 4 · LEN 10

  6. 6

    Run the cycle

    Design, instrument, evaluate, refine — then report, in first person, the framing the evidence forced you to revise. An iteration that revised nothing has to argue why.

    Iteration cycle report LEN 1 · Assignment 2

  7. 7

    Make it survive

    Plan adoption and sustainment against the cost of owning the thing, not the cost of building it, and design the support layer so it runs when you are not in the room.

    Implementation and sustainment plan, job aids LEN 6 · LEN 10

  8. 8

    Defend it

    Brief the people who will act on it — technical and non-technical in the same room — stating the uncertainty and the limits of what you can infer.

    Reproducible report, governance plan, 12-minute briefing LEN 1 · LEN 10

Coming across from LXD or AILE

If you come from learning experience design

Carries over The learning sciences, needs analysis, and design craft. LENS assumes them — the Learning Sciences Studio is a shared foundation course, and production of content and media stays with LXD.

What's new The unit of analysis moves outward: from the experience to the operational system that hosts it. You write capability requirements decomposed from a system requirement, and you measure against operational outcomes rather than completion and satisfaction.

The move you'll learn Telling a learning problem from a system problem — and being willing to report that the intervention you were asked for is not the fix.

If you come from AI leadership in education

Carries over Fluency with the tools, and the governance, adoption, and organizational questions that come with them. LENS treats AI as one case of human-system collaboration, not a separate subject.

What's new You specify the partnership as an engineered object: role architecture, authority gradients, mode and state transparency, recoverability — and delegation with revocation, where you name in advance the evidence that would take the delegation back.

The move you'll learn Measuring the human-machine team as a unit of analysis, and specifying capability requirements for systems that have not been fielded yet, from design artifacts alone.

What changes between arriving and leaving

The concentration's claim is the delta, not the starting point. Every pair below is assessable from course and capstone artifacts.

Attribute a performance problem to training by default.
Diagnose whether the gap is rooted in learning, in system design, or in organizational conditions — before proposing anything.
Interpret simple descriptive data about learners — completion, scores — as someone else presents it.
Instrument a learning environment ethically and produce decision-grade evidence: dashboards and reproducible reports linking learning measures to operational performance.
Recognize that learning solutions sit inside larger organizational and technical systems.
Map capabilities and conditions in the learning environment and its host system, translating capability requirements across the human-machine interface into design requirements.
Identify stakeholders in a familiar setting and describe their needs informally.
Run a stakeholder and context analysis in a high-consequence environment using a human systems integration framework, producing a documented context model.
Apply what you know about how people learn within one professional tradition.
State each design choice as a testable hypothesis and carry it through an instrumented cycle.
Use AI tools as a consumer.
Design capability development for human-AI teams and specify learning requirements for systems not yet fielded.
Work inside your own professional silo, handing off at its boundary.
Lead and integrate a learning engineering team across disciplines, with the shared vocabulary the work requires.

Where the value lands

Money not spent on the wrong fix

Training is the default answer to a performance problem, and often the wrong one. A graduate who can attribute the gap — with evidence — stops the spend that was never going to work.

Evidence a decision-maker can act on

Not completion rates. Dashboards, reproducible reports, and governance documentation that state what is known, what is assumed, and what would change the decision.

Capability specified before it is late

Requirements written from design artifacts, before a system is fielded — so capability is designed into the lifecycle rather than bolted on when training arrives last.

Asked in every session

Do I need to code?
No. Computational and AI methods are a recommended elective, not a gate. The required methods courses teach you to produce and appraise evidence, and to brief the specialist where depth is needed.
Do I have to work in healthcare or defense?
No. Those are two of seven representative domains, and the list is not exhaustive. Education is on it as a domain and is also the discipline LENS works from.
Is this a research degree?
No. Study design and human-subjects depth are treated at awareness and routed to an elective. The capstone is applied work in a real organization.
