Fictional workshop · Planning practice

Make a plan you can explain.

Inspect the available resources, propose an activity, and compare your proposal with the evidence. If a resource or helper becomes unavailable, revise the plan or ask for the information you need.

This is a reflection tool. It does not award a score, verify a plan, or establish that learning occurred.

1. Inspect the applied case

2. Propose a plan or ask a question

Explain which available resources your activity needs, how it meets the constraints, and what you would do if the main plan cannot work. Use only the participant needs that are supplied.

You may write freely. Explicit references such as F1 and C1 help connect the proposal to the evidence. Optional headings are Plan, Resources, Constraints, Support, Fallback, and Questions. A question about a missing fact is a valid next step.

Revise and Skip keep your draft. Reset clears your draft and attempt count while keeping the applied case and the case editor.

3. Review the evidence and revise

Replace or change the case

Use fictional information. The JSON must contain title and task as strings, plus facts and constraints as arrays of strings. The page displays their contents as data.

To introduce a change, edit an availability or support fact and apply it. Your draft stays in place; previous feedback is cleared. Source IDs are assigned from the newly applied lists, so an ID may refer to different text after a change. Unapplied edits do not change the active case.

The page does not transmit entries or save progress for later sessions. Keep a separate copy if you want to retain your proposal or edited case.
